Frequently asked questions

Is FreeResizer really free?

Yes. Every tool is free and there is no account, trial or hidden limit. The site is supported by advertising, which keeps the tools free for everyone.

Are my photos uploaded to a server?

No. Images are opened and processed inside your browser. Your files are not sent to us and are not stored on our servers.

Do I need to create an account?

No. Open the site, choose a tool and start working. No email address or password is needed.

Which file formats are supported?

JPG, PNG, WebP, GIF and BMP images are supported. Results can be saved as JPG, PNG or WebP, and photos can be combined into a PDF or exported from one.

Can I use the tools on my phone?

Yes. The site is responsive and works in current mobile browsers on Android and iPhone.

Do the tools add a watermark?

No watermark is ever added automatically. The Watermark tool only adds one when you choose to.

How large can my images be?

Most photos from a phone or camera work without trouble. Extremely large files, such as stitched panoramas or scans above roughly 100 megapixels, can run out of memory on a phone and fail to open. In that case resize the original on a computer first.

Can I use the results commercially?

Yes. You keep the rights to your own images and to the files you create with these tools. We do not add watermarks, claim ownership or receive your files.

About FreeResizer

Reviewed 9 November 2026

FreeResizer is a simple, free set of browser-based image tools built for people who just need a picture to be the right size, the right format or a bit smaller. No sign-up, no software and no uploads.

The project started as a small resize tool for the many forms and social networks that ask for very specific dimensions. It grew into a full toolkit for everyday image and document jobs.

What we offer

  • Resize images by pixels or percentage
  • Compress to a target file size in KB or MB
  • Convert between JPG, PNG and WebP
  • Add text or logo watermarks to images
  • Pick colors from any image
  • Clean up photos of documents with the scanner
  • Convert photos to PDF and PDF pages to JPG

Our approach to privacy

Your images are processed inside your own browser using the HTML canvas and file APIs. They are not uploaded to or stored on our servers. Read our Privacy Policy for details about cookies and advertising.

How we are supported

FreeResizer is supported by advertising, which lets us keep every tool free. Ads are kept clearly separate from the tools and content, and they never change how your files are processed.

Who runs freeresizer

freeresizer is an independent project maintained by a small team of one. It is not a company product, an agency service or a marketplace. The site exists because the alternative — uploading personal photos to an unknown server just to change their dimensions — never sat right.

How the tools are tested

Every tool is checked with real files before release, on desktop and on a phone. Test files cover each accepted format, transparent PNGs, very tall and very wide images, and photos with EXIF rotation. A tool is only listed on the home page when it produces a correct, downloadable file.

The written guides are tested the same way: every step refers to a control that exists in the live interface, with the same wording a visitor sees. Platform dimensions and upload limits are checked against current help pages and revisited when a platform announces a change.

Editorial standards and corrections

Guides are written for everyday users and avoid advice that cannot be verified. Where a number depends on the device — file sizes, memory limits, DPI equivalents — the guide explains the reasoning instead of presenting a single magic value. Each guide shows the date it was last reviewed.

We do not publish scraped articles, auto-generated filler or pages that exist only to repeat a keyword. Every guide is written for a specific job, links to the tool it describes, and is corrected or removed when it stops being accurate.

If something is wrong, out of date or confusing, tell us on the Contact page. Corrections are published in the guide itself, not buried in a changelog.

How the site changes

The toolkit is deliberately small. In November 2026 the standalone Compress tool was retired and target-size compression was kept in Resize to KB, so there is one clear place to do each job. The guide set was rewritten at the same time around the eight tools that remain, and the guides were reviewed together so their numbers agree.

Tools are not added to fill a grid. A new tool is published only when it does something the existing set cannot, and it gets a written guide before it is linked from the home page. When a guide is corrected, the review date on that page changes; the sitemap dates follow the same review.
